### Q21. Tell me about a time you turned around a failing technology initiative

**Answer:**
**Situation:** A cloud migration program for a large BFSI client was 4 months behind schedule, 40% over budget, and the client was considering termination.

**Root Causes Identified:**

- No clear architecture governance – teams making conflicting technology choices
- Monolithic lift-and-shift approach instead of right-sizing for cloud
- Insufficient cloud expertise in the delivery team
- Poor communication between onshore and offshore teams

**Actions Taken:**

1. Conducted a 2-week assessment and created a transparent status report for the client
2. Restructured the program into smaller, independently deliverable workstreams
3. Brought in 5 certified cloud architects and implemented pair programming
4. Established daily war-room calls with clear action items and owners
5. Re-baselined the plan with realistic milestones and built-in buffers
6. Implemented automated deployment pipelines to accelerate delivery

**Results:**

- Delivered the remaining scope in 3 months (vs. original 6-month revised estimate)
- Reduced cloud costs by 35% through right-sizing and reserved instances
- Client renewed for a 3-year managed services contract
- Team's cloud certification rate went from 20% to 85%

### Q22. How do you handle disagreements with other C-suite executives on technology decisions?

**Answer:**
**My approach: "Data over opinions, alignment over agreement."**

1. **Listen first:** Understand the other executive's perspective, concerns, and success metrics
2. **Find common ground:** Identify shared business objectives that both perspectives serve
3. **Present data:** Use benchmarks, case studies, and financial analysis to support my recommendation
4. **Offer options:** Present 2-3 alternatives with clear trade-offs (cost, time, risk, quality)
5. **Propose a pilot:** When disagreements persist, suggest a time-boxed experiment to validate assumptions
6. **Commit fully:** Once a decision is made, align 100% regardless of my initial position

**Example:** If a CEO wants to adopt a cheaper but less scalable technology to save costs, I would:

- Quantify the Total Cost of Ownership including technical debt and re-platforming costs
- Show a 3-year comparison demonstrating long-term savings of the scalable option
- Propose a phased approach that manages short-term budget constraints while building toward the right architecture

### Q25. Explain how you would design a cloud migration strategy for a legacy enterprise client

**Answer:**
**My 6R Framework approach:**

1. **Rehost (Lift & Shift):** For applications that need quick migration with minimal change
2. **Replatform (Lift & Optimize):** Move to managed services (e.g., RDS instead of self-managed DB)
3. **Refactor:** Re-architect for cloud-native (microservices, containers, serverless)
4. **Repurchase:** Replace with SaaS alternatives where appropriate
5. **Retain:** Keep on-premises for regulatory or latency requirements
6. **Retire:** Decommission unused applications

**Execution methodology:**

- **Discovery:** Application portfolio assessment with dependency mapping
- **Wave Planning:** Group applications by dependency, risk, and business priority
- **Landing Zone:** Build secure, compliant cloud foundation (networking, identity, governance)
- **Migration Factory:** Standardized tooling and processes for repeatable migrations
- **Validation:** Automated testing, performance benchmarking, and security verification
- **Optimization:** Post-migration right-sizing, cost optimization, and modernization
